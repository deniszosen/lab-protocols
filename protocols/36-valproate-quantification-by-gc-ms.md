## Purpose

Measure valproate (VPA) in whole-brain homogenates by liquid extraction, derivatisation and gas chromatography-mass spectrometry, to build brain concentration-time curves (protocol 39).

## Before you start

- This is analytical chemistry. If your group has no GC-MS, work with a core facility or a bioanalytical laboratory and agree the method with them first.
- Samples come from chicken embryos dosed as in protocol 10 and collected as in protocol 11. The animal permit belongs to the original study (FOTS 13896). You need your own approval to repeat the work.
- Use cold equipment and a fume hood for hexane, hydrochloric acid and the derivatisation reagent.

## Materials

- VPA standard solutions at 1, 10, 50, 100 and 500 µM. Control solutions at 60 and 300 µM.
- Internal standard: cyclohexanecarboxylic acid, 800 µM (and 1600 µM for the higher internal standard level).
- 0.2 M hydrochloric acid, hexane, cold MQ water.
- Derivatisation reagent: N-methyl-N-(trimethylsilyl)trifluoroacetamide (MSTFA).
- Untreated chicken brain homogenate, for matrix-matched standards.
- Pellet pestle, centrifuge cooled to 4 °C, autosampler vials.
- Agilent HP 6890 gas chromatograph with an Agilent HP 5973 MSD mass spectrometer and an electron ionisation source.

## Procedure

1. Homogenise each whole brain in cold MQ water with a pellet pestle. The volume in µL is twice the brain weight in mg.
2. **Make the standards in the same matrix.** Add 50 µL of each standard VPA solution to 50 µL of untreated chicken brain homogenate.
3. To 100 µL of sample homogenate, add 50 µL of 800 µM cyclohexanecarboxylic acid as the internal standard.
4. Add 1 mL of 0.2 M hydrochloric acid and 500 µL of hexane to extract VPA into the organic phase. Mix well.
5. Centrifuge at 4500 rpm for 10 minutes at 4 °C.
6. Move 200 µL of the organic phase to an autosampler vial.
7. Add 50 µL MSTFA and let the vials stand for 15 minutes.
8. Analyse on the GC-MS.
9. Calculate VPA in each sample from the calibration curve. Include the 60 and 300 µM controls in every run.
