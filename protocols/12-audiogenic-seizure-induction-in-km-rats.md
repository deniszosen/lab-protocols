## Purpose

Induce audiogenic seizures in a genetically seizure-prone rat strain and collect the striatum and substantia nigra at defined stages of a seizure for protein analysis.

## Before you start

- This procedure causes seizures in live animals. It needs a project approved by the responsible animal welfare authority, with a justified use of animals and humane endpoints. The original study followed the European Convention for the Protection of Vertebrate Animals used for experimental and other scientific purposes. See chapter 11 of the [lab handbook](https://github.com/deniszosen/lab-handbook).
- The lab prefers human cell and tissue models where they can answer the question (chapter 7).

## Animals

Inbred Krushinsky-Molodkina (KM) rats with inherited audiogenic epilepsy. They respond to the stimulus with clonic-tonic seizures with 99% probability once the readiness is formed, at the end of the third month of life. The study used rats older than 3 months with a body weight of 250 to 300 g, housed individually under natural lighting with free access to food and water. Each group had five rats.

## Procedure

1. **Test readiness.** Two weeks before the experiment, expose every rat to the acoustic stimulus to check its seizure readiness.
2. **Stimulus.** The paper states an acoustic stimulus "with the intensity of 50 dB and frequency of 10 kHz, produced by a sound generator". Confirm the intensity and its reference with the source laboratory before you use it. Note, not from the paper: 50 dB looks low for audiogenic seizure induction, and the paper gives no reference level, so the printed figure may be a typo or may refer to a different scale.
3. **Record** the presence and latency of the audiogenic seizure, wild running, clonic and tonic seizures, and ataxia.
4. **Groups.** Control (no stimulus, taken out of the experiment). Clonic-tonic seizure group (stimulated and decapitated after the onset of clonic-tonic seizures). Ataxia group (decapitated after the onset of the ataxia stage). All three groups are processed on the same day.
5. **Dissect.** Divide the brain into two hemispheres in the sagittal plane. From one hemisphere, dissect the striatum and the substantia nigra for western blot (protocol 26).
6. **Fix the second hemisphere** in 4% paraformaldehyde dissolved in phosphate buffer pre-cooled to 4 °C, for immunohistochemistry (protocol 22).

## Statistics as reported

Student's t-test or the Mann-Whitney U-test, with n equal to the number of animals per group (five), mean ± SD, p < 0.05.

## Not stated in the paper

- the sound level reference, duration of the stimulus and the test chamber
- how the animals were killed beyond "decapitated", and the dissection details
- blinding and how the stage of seizure was scored
